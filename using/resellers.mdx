Customers answers **"who are my customers, and what can they send?"** A reseller runs
OMNI for businesses of its own. Each customer is a full OMNI account, under the
reseller's [brand](/using/branding). It sends with [units](/using/units) the reseller
gives it; each message is charged to the reseller at the reseller's price.

**Who can use it:** the **Owner** or **Reseller** role.

**Plan:** the account must be a reseller. FireFlo turns this on; until it does, the
screen explains that the account isn't set up as a reseller.

<Note>
  Resellers set no prices and no markup. FireFlo gives a reseller money and a price list;
  the reseller gives its customers units. What it charges them for those units is between
  them.
</Note>

## Your customers

At the top of the page:

- **You sell units**: 1 unit = 1 message, per channel and product type.
- **Your pockets pay**: the money that pays for every message your customers send, at your
  prices. For SMS on FireFlo's connection, that is your SMS balance at the gateway.
- **Units you can still give**: worked out from that money and your price in your home
  country, after the units already with customers.

If your money runs out on a channel, the page says that every customer's sends there are
paused. If it no longer covers the units your customers hold at your current prices, it
warns you. Nothing is taken back.

**Unit history** lists the units you gave and took back, and the units you used yourself.

The table lists each customer with its status, one column for each kind of unit any of
them holds (**OK**, **Low** or **None · sends paused**), and its **Fee wallet**: the money
that pays its plan and battery fees. Filter by status (*Pending approval*, *Trial*,
*Active*, *Payment due*, *Suspended*, *Low balance*) or search by name.

### As a sub-reseller

A sub-reseller sees **Your units · received from your provider** instead: for each kind,
what it **passed to customers** and what **you hold**. It can pass on only what it holds,
and asks its own provider for more. It has no pockets and no prices.

## Set up a customer

<Steps>
  <Step title="Start">
    Choose **New customer**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Who they are">
    Enter the **company**, and the **owner's email** and **name**. The owner is the first
    person who can sign in, and is emailed a link to set a password.
  </Step>
  <Step title="What they are on">
    Pick the **product** (plan), the **trial** in days before they owe anything (0 for
    none), and an optional **opening credit** from your balance. Opening credit goes into
    their fee wallet, for plan and battery fees.
  </Step>
  <Step title="How they send SMS">
    Decide whether they **may connect their own SMS gateway**. If not, SMS goes through
    the gateway you sell them, and the choice is hidden from them. Where your SMS runs on
    FireFlo's connection, the choice isn't offered: an SMS login is made for the customer
    by itself.
  </Step>
  <Step title="A reseller of its own?">
    Turn on **A reseller: passes units on to customers of its own** to make the customer a
    sub-reseller.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Create">
    Choose **Create customer**. They send with the units you give them afterwards.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Anyone who signs up at your own web address also becomes your customer. See
[Branding](/using/branding).

## Give units

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open Give units">
    Select **Give units** on the customer's row, or on its **Units** tab.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Choose the kind and amount">
    Choose the **Channel** and **Product type**, and enter the **Units**. Add a **Note**
    if you like, such as an invoice number; your customer sees it.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Give">
    Select **Give N units**. The panel beside it says what the customer holds now and
    after, and how many you can still give.
  </Step>
</Steps>

No money moves when you give units. Each message the customer sends uses one unit and is
charged to your money then.

**Take back unused units**, in the same window, takes back units the customer hasn't
used. You can't take back more than it holds.

You can't give units for a kind that has no price for you yet. Units can't be used for
another channel or product type.

## One customer

Open a customer to manage them.

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Overview">
    - **What they're on**: change their product, and see their fee wallet, when their
      trial ends and how many people they have.
    - **How they send**: allow or stop their own SMS gateway, and make them a sub-reseller.
    - **Suspend**: nobody at the customer can sign in until you **let them back in**.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Units">
    **Their units**, each kind with its status, **Give units**, and **Their unit history**.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Sending">
    **Allowed countries**: where they may send, on every channel. Choose **Every country
    you can send to**, or **Only these countries**. You can choose only from the countries
    your own account may send to. A message to a number anywhere else is refused before
    it is sent, and isn't charged.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Fee wallet">
    - **Add credit** moves credit from your balance to their fee wallet, which pays their
      plan and battery fees. Give an amount and a reference; the reference shows on both
      ledgers.
    - **Low-balance warnings**: the line below which they and you are told their wallet
      is running low.
    - **Your terms**: a note only you see, and whether they can sign in.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Team">
    **Their people**: who can sign in, their roles, when they last signed in, and
    invitations still pending. They run their own team.
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

Credit you move shows on your [Transactions](/using/billing#transactions) as *Credit
allocated*, and on theirs as *Credit received*.

## What a customer can and can't do

- A customer runs its own account: its team, contacts, channels and messages.
- A customer can never do more than you can. Its roles are limited to what your account
  may do, and its allowed countries to yours.
- A customer sees its units, not your prices.

FireFlo's side of running resellers is described in
[For operators: Resellers](/operators/resellers).
